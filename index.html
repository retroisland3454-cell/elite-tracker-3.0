Day A — Push
Chest · Shoulders · Triceps · Monday
This is your width day. Shoulders and chest create the V-taper. Every rep of lateral raises here is directly building the sharp silhouette. Focus on feeling the muscle, not just moving the weight.
1
Barbell Bench Press
4 sets × 8 reps · 2–3 min rest
⌄
Setup
Lower bar
Press up
How to do it
1
Lie flat on the bench. Eyes directly under the bar. Grip slightly wider than shoulder width — about a thumb's length from the knurling.
2
Set your back. Slight natural arch in lower back, shoulder blades squeezed together and pressed into the bench. Feet flat on the floor.
3
Unrack the bar. Take a big breath in, brace your core like you're about to get punched. Bar over your chest not your face.
4
Lower slowly — 2 seconds down — to lower chest/nipple line. Elbows at about 45–75° from your torso. Not flared out wide, not pinned to your sides.
5
Press up and slightly back toward the rack. Exhale on the way up. Don't bounce the bar off your chest.

2
Dumbbell Lateral Raises
4 sets × 15 reps · 60 sec rest · ⭐ Most important
⌄
Start
Halfway
Top (shoulder height)
How to do it
1
Stand with dumbbells at your sides. Slight bend in elbows — never lock them straight. Light weight only, 10–15 lbs to start.
2
Tilt your torso very slightly forward — about 10°. This puts the side delt in better position to do the work.
3
Raise both arms out to the side in a wide arc. Lead with your elbows, not your wrists. Think of pouring water out of a jug — pinky slightly higher than thumb at the top.
4
Stop at shoulder height. Not higher. Going above shoulder height shifts the load to your traps and takes it off the side delt — the exact muscle you're training.
5
Lower slowly — 3 seconds down. The lowering phase builds as much muscle as the raising phase. Don't drop the weights.

This is the #1 exercise for shoulder width. Use lighter weight than you think. If you're shrugging your shoulders up, the weight is too heavy. 15 controlled reps beats 8 sloppy ones every time.

3 sets × 10 reps · 90 sec rest
⌄
Start position
Press overhead
How to do it
1
Sit on a bench with back support. Hold dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing forward, elbows at 90°.
2
Brace your core — don't let your lower back arch excessively. Keep your ribcage down.
3
Press both dumbbells straight up until arms are fully extended. Dumbbells come together slightly at the top — don't clang them.
4
Lower back to shoulder height in 2 seconds. Elbows should be at or slightly below 90° at the bottom.

4
Face Pulls (Cable, Rope)
3 sets × 20 reps · Every session · 🔑 Posture fix
⌄
Arms extended
Pull to face
How to do it
1
Set the cable machine to face height with a rope attachment. Stand back far enough that there's tension in the cable before you start.
2
Grab the rope with both hands, palms facing inward. Step back into a split stance for balance. Light weight — this is not a strength exercise.
3
Pull the rope toward your face, separating your hands as you pull. Elbows flare high and wide — think of pulling it apart like you're tearing it in two.
4
Squeeze your rear delts and rhomboids at the end of the movement. Hold for 1 second. This squeeze is the whole point.
5
Return slowly — 2 seconds back out. Feel the stretch in the rear delt as you extend.

Day B — Pull
Back · Biceps · Rear Delts · Wednesday
This is your depth day. A thick back creates presence from every angle. Pull with your back first — your arms are just hooks. If you feel this in your biceps more than your back, your form needs work.
1
Barbell Bent-Over Row
4 sets × 8 reps · 2–3 min rest
⌄
Hinge, arms down
Row to belly
How to do it
1
Stand over the bar, feet shoulder width. Grip just outside your legs, overhand grip.
2
Hinge at the hips — back flat, torso 45° to horizontal. Think of pushing your hips back, not bending your knees down.
3
Pull the bar to your lower belly/belly button. Drive your elbows back behind you. Think elbows, not hands.
4
Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top. Hold 1 second. Lower slowly back to full hang.

3
Hammer Curl
3 sets × 12 reps · 60 sec rest
⌄
Start (thumbs up)
Curl up
How to do it
1
Hold dumbbells with a neutral grip — thumbs pointing up, like you're holding a hammer. Keep this grip the entire movement.
2
Pin your elbows to your sides. They should not move forward or back during the curl. Elbows are the hinge point.
3
Curl both dumbbells up simultaneously. Or alternate — both methods work. Squeeze at the top.
4
Lower slowly — full extension at the bottom. Don't let your elbows swing forward to get more range.
BrachialisBrachioradialisBiceps

Day C — Legs
Quads · Hamstrings · Glutes · Calves · Friday
Most people skip legs. Don't. Heavy leg training produces more testosterone than any other muscle group. That testosterone drives upper body growth. Trained legs also make your entire physique look proportional.
1
Barbell Back Squat
4 sets × 8 reps · 2–3 min rest · 👑 King lift
⌄
Setup
Halfway
Parallel depth ✓
How to do it
1
Bar on your upper traps — just below the bony ridge at the top of your shoulder blades. Not on your neck. Hands grip the bar just outside shoulder width.
2
Feet shoulder width, toes pointed out 15–30°. Take a big breath in and brace your core hard before you unrack.
3
Sit back and down — not just straight down. Push your knees out over your toes. Your hips go back first, then down. Keep chest tall.
4
Reach parallel — hips at or below knee height. This is the minimum. Half squats are leaving muscle on the table.
5
Drive through your heels to stand. Push the floor away. Exhale on the way up. Knees stay tracking over toes the entire time.

Romanian Deadlift (RDL)
3 sets × 10 reps · 90 sec rest
⌄
Standing start
Hip hinge — flat back
How to do it
1
Stand holding the bar at hip level, hands just outside your hips, overhand grip. Slight bend in knees — not a squat, not straight-legged.
2
Hinge at your hips, pushing them back behind you as the bar slides down your legs. Back stays flat — imagine a table on your back that can't be disturbed.
3
Lower until you feel a deep stretch in your hamstrings — usually mid-shin level. Don't round your back to go deeper than your flexibility allows.
4
Drive hips forward to stand. Squeeze your glutes at the top. Bar stays close to your legs the entire movement.

Day D — Athletic
Deadlift · Traps · Neck · Full body · Saturday
This is the sharpness day. Not about maximum weight — about athleticism, posture, and the conditioning that makes you move like a built man. The deadlift and shrugs here build presence.
1
Conventional Deadlift
4 sets × 5 reps · 3 min rest · 👑 King lift
⌄
Setup — bar over mid-foot
Mid pull
Lockout — stand tall
How to do it
1
Walk up to the bar. Bar over mid-foot — about an inch from your shins. Feet hip width, toes slightly out.
2
Hinge and grip. Push hips back, grab the bar just outside your legs. Arms straight. Set your back — chest up, slight arch in lower back, lats tight. Big breath in, brace hard.
3
Push the floor away. The first part of the deadlift is a leg press — push the floor away from you, don't try to yank the bar up. Bar drags up your shins.
4
Once bar passes your knees, drive your hips forward to the bar. Squeeze your glutes hard at the top. Stand completely tall — no hyperextension.
5
Lower under control — hinge back at the hips. Don't drop the bar. Every rep starts from a dead stop on the floor.

✗ Don't
Yank bar up with your back
✓ Do
Bar stays close to legs all the way
✗ Don't
Let bar drift forward
✓ Do
Full lockout, squeeze glutes
✗ Don't
Hyperextend lower back at top
✓ Do
Dead stop each rep
✗ Don't
Bounce reps off the floor
HamstringsGlutesErectorsTrapsLatsQuads
The deadlift builds every single muscle in your posterior chain — the entire back side of your body. A man with a strong deadlift has thickness and presence from every angle. Start light, learn the pattern, add weight weekly.

Dumbbell Shrugs
4 sets × 15 reps · 60 sec rest · Trap builder
⌄
Start — shoulders down
Shrug up — hold 1 sec
How to do it
1
Stand holding heavy dumbbells at your sides. Arms straight, shoulders relaxed down. This is the starting position.
2
Shrug your shoulders straight up toward your ears as high as possible. Don't roll them forward or back — straight up.
3
Hold at the top for 1–2 seconds. Squeeze your traps hard. This is what builds the trap muscle.
4
Lower slowly — full depression of the shoulders at the bottom. Get a full stretch between reps.

Cardio
Tuesday + Thursday · 25 minutes
One protocol only. Incline treadmill walking. Not running, not HIIT. This specific method burns fat without spiking cortisol or competing with muscle recovery.
1
Incline Treadmill Walk
3.5 mph · 12–15% incline · 25 minutes
⌄
Walk — don't hold the rails
Protocol
1
Set incline to 12–15%. This is steep. It will feel hard. That's the point.
2
Set speed to 3.5 mph. Comfortable walking pace. You should be able to hold a conversation but breathing harder than normal.
3
Do not hold the handrails. Holding the rails reduces the calorie burn by 30-40% and defeats the purpose. Swing your arms naturally.
4
Walk for 25 minutes. You should be sweating. This burns approximately 250–300 calories without the cortisol spike that running causes.
✓ Do
Arms swing naturally
✗ Don't
Hold the rails at all
✓ Do
Upright posture, chin level
✗ Don't
Lean forward on the machine
This protocol is specifically chosen because it burns fat without raising cortisol. Running and HIIT are catabolic — they eat muscle. Incline walking preserves your muscle gains while burning the fat covering them.
Core · Home
No equipment · Any day · 15 minutes
Abs become visible when your body fat drops. Core work is about posture, stability, and protecting your spine under heavy lifts — not about burning belly fat. Do these at the end of any gym session or at home.
1
Dead Bug
3 sets × 10 reps each side · Best core exercise
⌄
Start — arms + legs up
Opposite arm + leg extend
How to do it
1
Lie on your back. Arms pointing straight at the ceiling. Legs bent at 90° — shins parallel to the floor.
2
Press your lower back flat into the floor. This is the key — your lower back must stay pressed down throughout the entire movement.
3
Slowly lower your right arm and left leg at the same time — opposite sides. Lower until they're just above the floor. Keep lower back flat the whole time.
4
Return to start, then repeat on the other side. Left arm and right leg. That's one rep each side.
✓ Do
Lower back flat on floor always
✗ Don't
Let your back arch up
✓ Do
Slow and controlled — 3 sec down
✗ Don't
Rush through the movement
Deep core (TVA)Rectus abdominisHip flexors
The dead bug is the single best core exercise for building the deep stability that protects your spine under squat and deadlift load. It looks easy. If your lower back comes off the floor, slow down.

Hollow Body Hold
3 sets × 20–30 seconds · Gymnast core
⌄
Hold this position
How to do it
1
Lie on your back, arms overhead. Press your lower back into the floor. Pull your ribs down — don't let them flare up.
2
Lift your legs and arms just a few inches off the floor. Keep legs straight and together. Arms by your ears.
3
Hold this position. Your body should look like a slightly curved banana. Breathe shallowly. Do not let your back arch.
4
Start with 10 seconds. Build to 30 seconds over weeks. If your back lifts off the floor, raise your legs higher until you build the strength.

Daily Posture
5 minutes · Every morning · Free gains
Posture is 50% of the sharp look. A built man who hunches reads soft. A lean man who stands upright commands a room before he speaks. Five minutes every morning changes this within 6–8 weeks.
1
Wall Angels
2 sets × 10 reps · Opens shoulders
⌄
W position
Slide arms up to Y
How to do it
1
Stand with your back against a wall. Head, upper back, and glutes all touching the wall. Feet a few inches from the wall.
2
Press your arms against the wall in a W shape — elbows bent, hands level with your head. Your arms must touch the wall the entire time.
3
Slowly slide your arms up the wall into a Y shape overhead. Keep everything touching the wall the whole time. This is harder than it sounds.
4
Slide back down to W. That's one rep. 10 reps slowly every morning.
Lower trapsRhomboidsSerratus anterior
If your lower back or arms can't stay against the wall during this movement, your posture issue is significant. That's fine — that's exactly why you're doing this. It will get easier over 2–3 weeks.
